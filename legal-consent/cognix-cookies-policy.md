## 2. What Are Cookies?

Cookies are small text files that are placed on your device (computer, smartphone, or tablet) by websites you visit. They are widely used to make websites work efficiently, remember your preferences, and provide information to website owners.

Here's a simple example of what a cookie looks like:

```
Name:    flare_session
Value:   a7f3d92b1c...
Domain:  .flareai.co
Expires: Session
```

When you visit Flare, our servers can read the cookies stored on your browser to recognize you, restore your session, and serve you a personalized experience.

Cookies are **not programs** — they cannot run code, carry viruses, or access other files on your device. They are simply small pieces of text data.

## 4. Why We Use Cookies

Here is a plain-English explanation of why each category of cookie exists:

### 4.1 Essential Cookies
These are the bare minimum required to keep Flare running. Without them, you wouldn't be able to log in, your session would break on every page, and the app wouldn't function at all. These cannot be turned off without breaking the platform.

**Examples of what these do:**
- Keep you logged in as you navigate between pages
- Store your authentication token securely
- Maintain your active session on the server
- Enable security protections like CSRF tokens

### 4.2 Preference Cookies
These cookies remember choices you've made so you don't have to re-configure things every time you visit.

**Examples of what these do:**
- Remember your preferred AI model (e.g., Claude, GPT-4o, Gemini)
- Remember your interface theme (light mode / dark mode)
- Remember your language and region settings
- Save your default conversation settings or system prompt preferences

### 4.3 Analytics Cookies
These cookies help us understand how people use Flare so we can make it better. All data collected via analytics cookies is **anonymized** — we cannot identify you personally from analytics data.

**Examples of what these do:**
- Track which features are used most often
- Understand which pages users visit and in what order
- Measure session duration and engagement
- Identify technical errors or crashes
- Help us understand what's working and what's not in the product

We use this data only internally for product improvement — never to profile you or for advertising.

### 4.4 Security Cookies
These cookies are used to protect your account and the platform from malicious activity.

**Examples of what these do:**
- Detect suspicious login attempts
- Prevent cross-site request forgery (CSRF) attacks
- Rate-limit brute force attempts on login forms
- Identify unusual account activity patterns

## 5. First-Party vs Third-Party Cookies

### 5.1 First-Party Cookies
First-party cookies are set directly by Flare (the `flareai.co` domain). These are the cookies we have full control over and are primarily described in this policy.

### 5.2 Third-Party Cookies

> **Important:** Flare does **not** use third-party advertising or retargeting cookies. We do not allow ad networks to place cookies on your device through our platform.

## 6. Session Cookies vs Persistent Cookies

### 6.1 Session Cookies
Session cookies are **temporary** and are deleted automatically when you close your browser. They are used to maintain your session while you're actively using Flare — for example, keeping you logged in as you navigate the app.

### 6.2 Persistent Cookies
Persistent cookies remain on your device for a set period of time (or until you delete them manually). They are used to remember your preferences across multiple visits — so you don't have to re-configure your settings every time you open Flare.

| Cookie Type | Lifespan | Example Use |
|---|---|---|
| Session | Deleted on browser close | Active login session |
| Persistent (short) | 7–30 days | Remember login state |
| Persistent (long) | Up to 1 year | Theme, language, model preference |

## 8. Local Storage & Session Storage

In addition to cookies, Flare also uses **browser local storage** and **session storage** — two similar technologies built into modern browsers.

| Technology | What It Is | How We Use It |
|---|---|---|
| **Local Storage** | Persistent key-value storage in your browser | Store UI preferences, cached settings, and app state between visits |
| **Session Storage** | Temporary key-value storage (cleared on tab close) | Store temporary app state during an active session |

Unlike cookies, local storage and session storage data is **never sent to our servers automatically**. It lives entirely in your browser and is only accessed by Flare's JavaScript code running in the tab.

**What we store in local storage:**
- Your interface preferences (sidebar state, font size, etc.)
- Cached conversation metadata for faster loading
- Draft messages you haven't submitted yet
- Temporary UI state

You can clear local storage at any time through your browser's developer tools or by clearing your browser's site data for `flareai.co`.

## 12. How to Manage & Disable Cookies

You have several ways to control cookies:

### 12.1 Flare Cookie Settings
You can update your cookie preferences at any time by clicking **"Cookie Settings"** in the footer of the Flare website. This will let you enable or disable non-essential cookie categories.

### 12.2 Browser Settings
You can configure your browser to block or delete cookies. Here's how in the most popular browsers:

| Browser | Cookie Settings Location |
|---|---|
| **Google Chrome** | Settings → Privacy and Security → Cookies and other site data |
| **Mozilla Firefox** | Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data |
| **Safari** | Preferences → Privacy → Manage Website Data |
| **Microsoft Edge** | Settings → Cookies and site permissions → Cookies and site data |
| **Opera** | Settings → Advanced → Privacy & security → Cookies |

You can choose to:
- Block all third-party cookies (recommended for privacy)
- Block all cookies (may break some functionality)
- Delete cookies when the browser closes
- Block cookies from specific sites

### 12.3 Browser Extensions
Privacy-focused browser extensions like **uBlock Origin**, **Privacy Badger**, or **Cookie AutoDelete** can give you more granular control over which cookies are allowed.

### 12.4 Opt-Out of Analytics
If you'd like to opt out of analytics tracking specifically:
- Use the Flare Cookie Settings panel to disable analytics cookies
- Or install the **Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on** if we use Google Analytics
- Or use a browser with built-in tracking protection (e.g., Firefox with Enhanced Tracking Protection)

## 13. Impact of Disabling Cookies

Here is what happens when you disable different categories of cookies:

| Cookie Type | Impact of Disabling |
|---|---|
| **Essential** | You will not be able to log in or use Flare. Core functionality will break. |
